3. The calculators

  • The budget planner and borrowing estimate are general calculators. They show what happens under the assumptions on the page, and you can change those assumptions.
  • Results are estimates, not forecasts. Small changes to assumptions, especially price growth, interest rates and investment returns, can change the results a lot. Property prices can fall as well as rise.
  • The borrowing figure is a rough guide, not a lender's assessment. Lenders use their own rules and may lend you less.
  • Amounts for future years are shown in today's dollars: they are scaled back by the inflation rate you set, so they compare with money today.
  • Tax, stamp duty, mortgage insurance and government scheme figures are simplified and may not reflect current rules or your situation.

4. The data

  • Sales, rents, addresses and boundaries come from government and open data sources. They are listed, with their licences, under "Data and licences" at the foot of the main page.
  • Government records can contain errors, omissions and delays. Sales usually appear six to eight weeks after settlement.
  • Medians and growth rates based on few sales can be misleading. Value estimates for an address are rough statistical estimates, not valuations, and must not be used in place of one.
  • We clean, combine and summarise the records, and we may correct or remove information at any time.
